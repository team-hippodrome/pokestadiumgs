# Fragment 64 model-update callback

## func_87F03D9C

`func_87F03F38` registers this callback with `func_80037E3C`, supplying its
newly allocated object as callback data. The registrar stores that pointer
at node offset 20 and invokes the callback with event 0. The graph renderer
later supplies event 2. The callback follows the object pointer and its
offset-32 auxiliary record.
//@src/fragments/64/fragment64_326820.c
//@src/340C0.c

For event 2 on a new `D_80094904` frame, the callback increments the object's
word counter at offset 28 and adds the auxiliary halfword at 30 to its
halfword at 28. A counter above the record's word threshold at offset 4
releases the object through `func_87F026E8(record->unk_08, object)`. That
helper clears the active flag and unlinks the item from its owning state.
Otherwise the three position floats at object offset 72 advance by the
three floats at offset 16, the signed rotation halfword at 70 advances by
the record's halfword at 20, and all three scale floats at 84 multiply by
the record's float at 24. The existing transform helper receives the matrix
at 132, current basis at `D_80094908 + 100`, position, angles and scale.
The cached frame at record offset 14 is updated on every path.
//@src/fragments/64/fragment64_324FB0.c
//@src/fragments/64/fragment64_328C20.c

The callback has a retail missing-result bug: `Geo_ProcessNodeChildren` and
`Geo_NodeReference` consume its result word, but the assembly has no explicit
result assignment. Returning the record pointer explicitly adds a move and
changes its carrier. The C retains the signed-word callback contract and
missing return, with `//!bug`; the constructor's old zero-argument void
forward declaration is corrected. Existing constructor and helper bodies
remain unchanged.
//@src/340C0.c

The accepted body matches all 80 instruction words (320 bytes) and its
56-byte frame. A named scale pointer used for all three multiplications and
the transform call reproduces the auxiliary-record spill at stack offset 44.
The direct-field form instead uses offset 40 and a 48-byte frame. No unused
local, extra storage, padding, fake, layout or instruction rewrite is added.
